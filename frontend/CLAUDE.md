# uptime-city: frontend

Web app that only displays the monitoring results served by the backend. It never sends commands, except the simulation buttons and the toolbox's regenerate button. See the root `CLAUDE.md` for the project overview and the REST/SSE contract with the backend.

## 3D engine: three.js

- Chosen over Babylon.js for these reasons:
  - Size: about 168 kB gzipped, against about 1.4 MB for Babylon.
  - A mature `WebGPURenderer` that falls back to WebGL2.
  - `OrthographicCamera` for the isometric view, and `InstancedMesh` to draw all buildings in one call.
  - A far bigger ecosystem.
- Babylon is a full game engine we don't need, and its React binding `react-babylonjs` is stale.

## Framework and tooling

- React with React Three Fiber (v9 stable; v10 makes WebGPU the default) and drei (`OrthographicCamera`, `Instances`, `Html`, `Text`).
- Vite and strict TypeScript.
- Live state lives in a **zustand** store fed by the SSE stream. 3D code reads it with `getState()` inside `useFrame`, so updates don't trigger React re-renders.
- **TanStack Query** handles the REST snapshot and history.
- Tests: Vitest with Testing Library for logic and the 2D panels and menus of the city. No Playwright end-to-end tests for now (see `.claude/rules/frontend-testing.md`).
- TypeScript API types are generated from the backend's `/openapi.json` with `openapi-typescript`.

## Map rule

- The ground of the 3D map is one continuous surface: never leave void between plates (neighbourhoods or wilderness). The size of a neighbourhood follows its size score (the host counts 20, each of its services 5, each vm-host 15 and each service on a vm-host 6): the bigger the score, the bigger the tile. The map is made of rows of neighbourhoods, as square as possible, and wild joining tiles fill what no neighbourhood covers. When tiles need more room, add joining tiles, never move them apart (see `src/city/world.ts` and its "no void" test).

## Biome rules

- A tile (neighbourhood or wild joining tile) has up to 2 biomes, with a transition between them, never a brutal change; the change between two neighbouring tiles is not brutal either.
- The biomes:
  - **Forest**: anywhere on the map, green, some trees here and there, can have little ponds or lakes.
  - **Mountain**: mostly at the center of the map, fir trees, frozen lakes, a bit of snow here and there.
  - **City**: anywhere on the map, mostly houses, car parks, shops and tennis courts.
  - **Coastal city**: only on the edges of the map, with the sea part of the tile always on the edge of the map, a beach and some coastal shops.
  - **Desert**: anywhere on the map, mostly sand and cacti, with a low chance of appearance.
  - **Tundra**: snow and a bit of vegetation, with a low chance of appearance.
- Houses stay sparse. Tennis courts: at most 4 per tile, always side by side, with a little club house against them. A city tile has up to one football stadium.
- The monitored buildings are always there as expected, whatever the biome.

How they are applied (constants in `src/city/biomes.ts`, `src/city/layout.ts` and `src/city/world.ts`):

- The main biome of a tile is drawn by weight from where it stands (`weights`): forest 3 and city 3 anywhere, mountain 0.2 at the edges up to 5.2 at the middle, desert 0.45, tundra 0.45, and coastal 7 but only on a tile touching the front edge of the map (+Z, towards the camera). The sea is only ever along that front edge, not the other three.
- 80% of the tiles have a second biome, on one side of the tile. 85% of the time it is the main biome of a neighbour of the same row, on that neighbour's side. A coastal second biome is only at the front or on a side, never at the back, and a coastal main biome never gives the front to another one.
- Inside a tile, the two biomes are dithered tile by tile over a wavy band across its middle (`secondShare`). Across the edge between two tiles, each tile takes some of its neighbour's biome over 4 tiles, the more the closer to the edge (`SEAM`, `blendSeams`). Trees on a tile that changes biome change kind with it.
- Each tile of ground carries its own biome, which decides its colours, its trees (oaks, pines and fruit trees, firs in the mountains, cacti in the desert, shrubs and a few firs in the tundra), its water (ponds, frozen lakes in the mountains and the tundra, none in the desert) and its relief (higher hills with snowy tops in the mountains, low dunes in the desert).
- Wild joining tiles have biomes too, chosen the same way, with a second one taken from the neighbourhood they touch. Their hills, trees and structures never hide a monitored building (`clearWilds`).
- City structures stand on city and coastal city ground. Away from the paths, only 1 house in 10 that comes up is built (`HOUSE_DENSITY`). The tennis club has 2 to 4 courts, at most one club per tile, and a road through it removes it whole. The stadium comes after the club, on 60% of the city tiles where it fits (`STADIUM_CHANCE`). The beach of a coastal city has kiosks, palms and parasols.
- The unmonitored decoration of a neighbourhood (houses, a supermarket, a car park) stays in every biome.

## Road rules

- There are normal roads (2 tiles wide, with a dashed middle line) and paths (narrower, without a middle line).
- Roads do not belong to one neighbourhood: they can run through several.
- Roads and paths can be horizontal or vertical; paths can have turns.
- Roads can cross each other, and where they do, there is a roundabout.
- Houses gather around paths preferably, not roads (but it can happen anyway).
- Monitored buildings gather around roads, with a low chance of being next to a path.

How they are applied (`layRoads` in `src/city/world.ts`, `layPath` in `src/city/layout.ts`, the traffic in `src/city/traffic.ts`):

- All roads are straight and lie on the edges between tiles. The roads of a neighbourhood run on along X, through the other tiles, as long as no building is in the way and no other road along X runs within 7 tiles. The front road of a row thus crosses the whole map.
- Roads along Z link roads along X, about one every 28 tiles across the map (`WAY_SPACING`), where nothing stands within 3.5 tiles of them. They run from one road along X to another, never into the sea, and hills beside them are levelled.
- Every place where a road along Z meets a road along X, at a crossing or at its ends, has a roundabout: a ring of road around a raised island with a tree. The middle line stops there.
- Cars drive along the roads of the whole map, one lane per direction, keeping to the same hand, through the plates and around the islands of the roundabouts. They never turn back: a car fades out where its lane ends (the edge of the map, or where the road stops), and the next one fades in at the start of the lane a few seconds later.
- Paths are 1 tile wide and leave a road, going straight for at least 3 tiles before they may turn at a right angle. They never run along another path or a road. They stop where they meet one, or after 10 to 25 tiles. Wild tiles have a wandering path or two.
- Houses are laid along the paths first, on city ground.
- Paths keep away from the monitored buildings, except for 15% of them, which get a path along their side (`PATH_BY_BUILDING`).

## Track rules

- One double track of ballast, sleepers and rails runs in a straight line from one edge of the map to the opposite one, near the back edge (-Z) or the left edge (-X), whichever strip has the fewest obstacles.
- It never goes over a monitored building, a roundabout or the sea. Trees, structures (a tennis club whole) and hills on it are removed or levelled, as for roads.
- A road it meets stays usable, with a level crossing and a few marks.
- Trains (a locomotive and carriages, passenger and freight, and about one in four a streamlined TGV; the trains go 2 times as fast as the cars, the TGV 5 times) run its whole length, one track for each way, coming in at one edge and going out at the other, fading in and out near the edges. One passes about every 60 seconds at the normal density of the vehicles, every 30 at twice, none at 0; the tracks always show.

How they are applied (`layTracks` in `src/city/tracks.ts`, the drawing in `src/city/trackBoxes.ts`, the trains in `src/city/trains.ts`):

- Each plate gets its part of the track in `layout.tracks`, drawn with it, and a nuked plate loses it.

## Airport rule

- The biggest neighbourhood has an airport: the bigger the tile, the longer the runway and the more stands (from a short runway with 2 stands to a long one with 4), scaled down to what fits, and left out if even the smallest does not fit.
- It never hides or removes a monitored building, no road, roundabout, water or rail runs under it (what else stands there makes way), and it keeps at least 8 tiles from every edge of the map.
- Planes (light tourism ones, cargo ones from a 4-tile runway, jumbo ones from 6, so every airport has all three) land and take off one at a time on the single runway, coming from and leaving beyond an edge of the map, climbing to and descending from a cruise height above the clouds (`cruiseHeight`). Other planes of all three kinds cross the sky from one edge of the map to another, at random and unrelated to the airport, at that same height, just above the clouds. The vehicles density scales the number of planes of both, and the vehicles effect hides them.

How they are applied (`placeAirport` in `src/city/airport.ts`, called by `worldLayout` after the roads and the tracks; the flights in `src/city/flights.ts`):

- Planes are placed after the group of the airport's plate, like the cars, and hidden when that plate is nuked. The planes of the sky (`src/city/overflights.ts`) are in the frame of the map, not of a plate, and hidden in focus view, like the clouds.

## Performance rules

- No real light per effect: every point light is evaluated for every lit fragment of the scene, and a change in their number recompiles the shaders. Fire has none, the floors glow red instead.
- One instanced mesh per building and effect, shared by its floors and hidden (`visible = false`) when nothing shows.
- Nothing is allocated or parsed in a `useFrame`: colors are made once (`src/city/effects/fireShape.ts`), event lists are read in loops (`src/city/effects/floorEvents.ts`), and work that depends on the state is done once per new state.

## Testing rule

- Do not check changes by rendering the page (browser, screenshots, dev server with mocked API) unless told to. Rely on typecheck and unit tests; the user checks the rendering by hand.

## Iterations

A lazy-loaded `<CityView>` canvas is the only view. The 2D view (cards and tabs) was removed.

The status→visual mapping and the city layout are pure functions, independent of the engine and unit-tested.

Runner-up: SvelteKit + Threlte. Rejected for its smaller community, fewer helpers than drei, and low 2026 activity.

- **Theme**: dark, in plain CSS with no framework. See `src/index.css`.
- **Simulation badge**: a magenta badge at the bottom left of the window tells when the backend simulates its statuses, with a counter to its right of the seconds left before they change, stopped at first (it shows the full period) and standing still until a click starts it (`POST /api/v1/simulation/start`), then counting down until a click stops it, with a small play or stop symbol at its top right corner for the action a click does, and no sync reload of the 3D view while it is stopped (`GET /api/v1/info`). See `src/ui/SimulationBadges.tsx`, `src/App.tsx`, `src/api/client.ts`, `src/index.css` and `tests/unit/App.test.tsx`.
- **Simulation controls**: while the simulation is stopped, a small arrow on top of the simulation badge rolls up from the badge (closed at first) and back down, like a blind, a narrow, tall box above it, in its colours and linked to the arrow without touching the badge row, with a button for each event that would otherwise happen by itself (reset everything up, nuke a host, demolish a VM, down services, revive a host, revive a VM, revive services, the two on services taking the number typed in the box), which reload the info and the statuses; the arrow and the box are gone while it runs. See `src/ui/SimulationBadges.tsx` (`SimulationControls`), `src/api/client.ts` (`actOnSimulation`), `src/index.css` and `tests/unit/SimulationControls.test.tsx`.
- **Simulation refresh button**: a round button right of the simulation timer redraws the simulated statuses at once, restarts the timer and reloads the data, the 3D view included (the one command the frontend sends, see the root `CLAUDE.md`). See `src/ui/SimulationBadges.tsx`, `src/api/client.ts` (`refreshSimulation`), `src/index.css` and `tests/unit/App.test.tsx`.
- **3D refresh**: the page reloads the statuses every 30s by default (in sync with the simulation when the backend simulates), and a box in the menus at the top right of the 3D view (whole map only, below the effects) picks 10s, 30s, 1min or 5min, or, in simulation only, a sync with the simulation's own refresh, and tells on the right of its title "in x seconds" before the next reload, while its "Refresh" title breathes to bold for 3 seconds as a reload begins. See `src/ui/RefreshSelector.tsx`, `src/ui/useHistoryRefresh.ts`, `src/App.tsx`, `tests/unit/RefreshSelector.test.tsx`, `tests/unit/useHistoryRefresh.test.tsx` and `tests/unit/App.test.tsx`.
- **Toolbox**: a toolbox icon at the bottom right of the 3D view (whole map only) opens a black box of actions, for now one button that regenerates the city: the backend draws and keeps a new seed (`POST /api/v1/city/regenerate`, read back with `GET /api/v1/city` before the city is built), and the splash screen covers the view (at least half a second) until the new city is built; the seed is mixed into every random draw of the layout, so the same seed lays the same city and an empty one the original. See `src/city/Toolbox.tsx`, `src/city/seed.ts`, `src/city/CityView.tsx`, `src/App.tsx`, `src/api/client.ts`, `tests/unit/Toolbox.test.tsx`, `tests/unit/seed.test.ts` and `tests/unit/App.test.tsx`.
- **Sliding names**: in the 2D panels over the 3D view (a building's floors, the services of a badge), a service name too long for its box slides to its end and back, pausing at both ends, so it can be read whole; with reduced motion it stays cut short with an ellipsis, and its tooltip shows it whole. See `src/ui/MarqueeText.tsx`, `src/ui/marquee.ts`, `src/city/BuildingPanel.tsx`, `src/city/StatusPanel.tsx`, `src/index.css` and `tests/unit/MarqueeText.test.tsx`.
- **Service graph**: a 📈 button next to the building icon of each service in the status panel (and on each floor of the building panel) opens a black box, with its top aligned with that panel, filling the room between the panel and the right menus (on the left of the building panel, to the edge of the view), graphing the response times of the checks we hold (the last 50): response time up, time across, one line that goes on through the outages, each of which is a red dotted vertical line (a failed check that has a response time keeps its point), and a tooltip with the time and response time of the check under the pointer; the box is moved by its title bar, resized by its bottom right corner, and shows one service at a time, closed by the button, its cross, or when its service leaves the list; plain SVG, no chart library; four cards under the graph tell the current status (a breathing UP or DOWN badge), the average response time, its range and how long ago the last check was. See `src/city/ServiceGraph.tsx`, `src/ui/useNow.ts`, `src/city/useServiceGraph.ts`, `src/domain/graph.ts`, `src/city/StatusPanel.tsx`, `src/city/BuildingPanel.tsx`, `tests/unit/graph.test.ts`, `tests/unit/ServiceGraph.test.tsx`, `tests/unit/StatusPanel.test.tsx` and `tests/unit/BuildingPanel.test.tsx`.
